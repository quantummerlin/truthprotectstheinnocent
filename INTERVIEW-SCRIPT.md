## BEFORE YOU BEGIN, PRINT & READ THIS FIRST

### Three Things to Remember in Every Answer:
1. **Speak only to what you personally witnessed or know**
2. **You are a character witness, not a lawyer, not a spokesperson**
3. **If in doubt, redirect: "What I can tell you is what I personally saw..."**

## OPENING, Who You Are

**Q: Who are you and how do you know Catherine?**

> "I am a close personal friend of Catherine's. I have known her for [X years / since we met in...]. I am speaking today as someone who knows this family personally, not as a representative or spokesperson for them."

## SECTION 1, Catherine as a Person and Mother

**Q: What kind of person is Catherine?**

> "Catherine is one of the most devoted, thoughtful mothers I have ever met. She makes every decision in her children's lives with extraordinary care. When I think of Catherine, I think of warmth, patience, and a deep commitment to her family."

---

**Q: What kind of mother is she?**

> "She is present, nurturing, and completely child-focused. Her children were her world. Every choice she and Nathan made, from where they lived, to how they educated the children, came from a place of love and deep intention. I never once had any concern about those children's wellbeing when they were with their parents."

---

**Q: Did you ever see anything that concerned you, any reason to worry about the children?**

> "Honestly, no. The children I knew were happy, healthy, loved and bonded with both their parents. I had no concerns whatsoever."

---

**Q: How would you describe her relationship with her children?**

> "It was beautiful. You could see she cherished every moment with them. The bond between Catherine and those children was evident to anyone who spent time with the family."

---

---

## SECTION 2, The Family's Lifestyle

**Q: The family lived off-grid / in an unconventional way. What did you make of that?**

> "Their lifestyle was intentional and thoughtful. Living simply, close to nature, these are choices many families make. It doesn't make someone a bad parent. The children were safe, loved, and thriving."

---

**Q: Were you ever worried the children weren't being looked after properly?**

> "Never. Catherine and Nathan were attentive, responsible parents. The children were well-fed, healthy, and clearly happy. There was nothing alarming about how they lived."

---

**Q: The children were homeschooled. Did that concern you?**

> "Not at all. Homeschooling is legal in Italy, it's called 'istruzione parentale', and the Italian Ministry of Education actually confirmed that the children were legally homeschooled. Thousands of families across Italy do the same. Catherine educated her children with care and intention."

---

---

## SECTION 3, The Separation and Its Impact

**Q: How has Catherine been since the children were taken?**

> "She is devastated. Any mother separated from her children would be. But what strikes me is her dignity in that pain. She has not given up. She has continued to fight for her children every single day."

---

**Q: How has this affected the family?**

> "This has been an unimaginable ordeal. No parent should have to go through this. I think about those children and what they must be experiencing, and it is heartbreaking. These children know their parents love them."

---

**Q: Do you believe the children were being harmed by their parents?**

> "Absolutely not. There was never any evidence of abuse or neglect, because there was none. The official reasons given for removing the children have since been disproven by Italian authorities themselves. I find the whole situation deeply distressing."

---

---

## SECTION 4, The Official Claims (Know These Facts, Refer to Them Calmly)

> **Note:** You don't need to be technical. These are just the facts. State them calmly and let them speak for themselves.

**Q: Weren't there concerns raised about vaccination, education and the home?**

> "Yes, those were the three reasons given by authorities. But here's what's important: all three have since been disproven by official Italian sources.
>
> The vaccinations, the regional health authority confirmed the children were vaccinated. In fact, they described the vaccinations as 'exaggerated,' meaning more than the minimum required.
>
> The education, the Ministry of Education confirmed the children were legally homeschooled under Italian law.
>
> The home, a certified professional assessor confirmed the structure was not at risk.
>
> I'm not a lawyer, but when the official reasons for removing children are disproven by official sources, questions have to be asked."

---

**Q: But there must have been a reason, authorities don't just take children for no reason.**

> "You'd like to think so. But in this case, the three specific reasons given have been officially disproven. I think that's worth sitting with. These aren't allegations I'm making, Italian authorities themselves produced records that contradict the original claims."

## SECTION 5, What You Want to See Happen

**Q: What do you want to come from speaking today?**

> "I want people to understand that behind this case is a real family, a mother, a father, and three children who are suffering every single day they are apart. I want this family reunited. That is all anyone who loves them wants."

---

**Q: What would you say to the authorities?**

> "I would ask them to look at this family honestly. Catherine and Nathan are good, loving parents. The evidence supports that. I would ask them to put the children's wellbeing first, and every expert who has assessed this case says those children need to be back with their parents."

**Q: What would you say to people watching at home?**

> "I would ask them not to judge a family based on incomplete information. Catherine chose a different way of life, a legal, thoughtful, loving way of life. Different doesn't mean wrong. And her children need their mother."

## REDIRECT PHRASES, Use These When You're Unsure

| Situation | What to Say |
|---|---|
| Legal question you can't answer | *"I'm not in a position to comment on legal proceedings. What I can tell you is what I personally witnessed."* |
| Asked to speculate | *"I don't want to speculate. What I know for certain is..."* |
| Asked about something you don't know | *"I don't have direct knowledge of that. What I do know is..."* |
| Pushed on a difficult point | *"I can only speak to my personal experience of this family."* |
| Asked a "trick" framing question | *"I wouldn't put it that way. What I would say is..."* |
| Feeling pressured or rushed | *"I want to make sure I answer this carefully, because accuracy matters."* |

---

---

## THINGS TO AVOID SAYING

- ❌ Do not name judges, social workers, or specific officials, keep focus on the situation, not individuals
- ❌ Do not speculate about motives: *"They did it because..."*
- ❌ Do not make legal statements: *"This is illegal..."* or *"They broke the law..."*
- ❌ Do not speak on behalf of the family officially: *"The family believes..."*, say *"in my personal view..."*
- ❌ Do not share any private details about the case that are not already public
- ❌ Do not get drawn into hypothetical questions: *"But what if..."*
- ❌ Do not express anger, calm, factual, and caring is far more powerful

>
> You cannot be "twisted" for speaking calmly and from personal knowledge.
