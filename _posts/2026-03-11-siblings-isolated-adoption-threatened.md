## What Is Happening Right Now

### The Children Are Being Isolated From Each Other

The three Birmingham-Trevallion siblings — Utopia, Galorian, and Blue Bell — have been separated from their parents since late 2025. Now, inside the institution, they are being **isolated from each other**.

This is not incidental. Sibling separation is one of the most psychologically damaging interventions in child welfare. It serves no protective purpose for children who have already been assessed as having a loving, safe family.

What it *does* serve: it creates distance between siblings. It erodes the shared bond that would make reuniting them — as a unit — the obvious and necessary outcome. And it makes the bureaucratic case for "they've adapted to their separate environments" easier to construct.

**A psychologist has already publicly documented that these children are suffering self-harm behaviours that did not exist before they were removed from their parents.** Now they are losing each other too.

---

### Adoption Is Permitted Under Italian Law — And Being Raised

Under **Italian Law 184/1983**, a court can declare a child to be in a *stato di abbandono* — a "state of abandonment" — even when parents are present, funded, and fighting for their children. The legal test is whether there is a "lack of moral and material assistance."

Once a declaration of adoptability (*adottabilità*) is issued:

- **Parental rights are permanently terminated**
- The child is legally severed from their biological family
- Reunification becomes legally impossible without overturning the declaration
- The process is designed to be irreversible

This is the machine that is being set in motion. Against a family whose three official justifications for removal have **all been disproven by Italian authorities themselves**.

## Frequently Asked Questions

These questions are appearing constantly in comments. Here are honest answers.

**"Why hasn't the Australian government brought them home?"**
Consular intervention in judicial proceedings of another sovereign country is legally complex. Governments can apply diplomatic pressure, raise formal concerns, and make the case internationally visible — but cannot overrule a foreign court. What they *can* do is escalate pressure significantly, and that escalation depends on political will, which depends on public pressure. That is where you come in.

**"Why doesn't the family just leave Italy?"**
The children are under a court order. Attempting to remove them without court authorisation would legally constitute abduction, even by their own parents. The family must work through the Italian legal system — which is why external pressure on Italian institutions matters.

**"How can adoption happen if the parents want their children?"**
Under Italian Law 184/1983, the legal standard is "state of abandonment" which the courts have significant discretion to define. Extended institutional care can itself be used as evidence that the children have adapted to a non-family environment. This is the machine that is now in motion, and it is why **acting now, before proceedings are formalised, is critical.**

**"Isn't this already in the highest courts?"**
The case has been reported to the Court of Cassation (Italy's highest court). As of the last update, no action had followed. International pressure makes inaction harder to sustain.

**"What would actually make Italy act?"**
Documented, escalating pressure from multiple directions simultaneously: diplomatic channels, parliamentary questions, sustained media coverage in multiple countries, and public correspondence that is too large to ignore. None of these alone is sufficient. All of them together can shift political will.
